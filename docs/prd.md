# Executive Summary

The Coastal Climate Risk Intelligence Dashboard is an AI-powered geospatial analytics platform designed to assess, visualize, and explain climate-related risks affecting coastal regions.

The platform integrates environmental datasets including weather observations, oceanographic information, satellite imagery, terrain models, and historical climate records to generate a comprehensive Climate Risk Index for coastal locations.

Unlike conventional weather dashboards that only display environmental conditions, this platform transforms heterogeneous environmental data into actionable intelligence through interactive visualizations, statistical analytics, and AI-generated advisory reports.

The primary objective is to assist governments, disaster management authorities, insurance providers, researchers, urban planners, and citizens in making informed, data-driven decisions regarding coastal climate resilience.

---

# Problem Statement

Coastal regions are increasingly vulnerable to climate-related hazards such as:

- Coastal flooding
- Sea level rise
- Storm surges
- Heavy rainfall
- Coastal erosion
- High tides
- Infrastructure damage

Although large volumes of environmental data are publicly available through government agencies and research organizations, these datasets remain fragmented across multiple platforms.

Stakeholders currently face several challenges:

- Lack of centralized climate intelligence
- Difficulty interpreting raw environmental datasets
- Limited hyper-local risk assessment
- Delayed disaster preparedness
- Limited explainability of risk indicators
- Absence of AI-assisted recommendations

There is a need for an integrated platform capable of transforming raw environmental observations into meaningful climate intelligence.

---

# Vision

To create an intelligent decision-support platform that enables proactive coastal risk management through the integration of geospatial analytics, statistical modeling, and artificial intelligence.

---

# Product Goals

The platform aims to:

- Aggregate climate-related datasets from multiple trusted sources.
- Generate hyper-local climate risk assessments.
- Visualize environmental risks through interactive GIS dashboards.
- Explain climate risks using AI-generated insights.
- Assist policymakers and disaster management teams in decision-making.
- Improve accessibility to environmental intelligence.

# Success Metrics

The MVP will be considered successful if it can:

- Integrate environmental datasets successfully.
- Generate climate risk scores.
- Display interactive GIS maps.
- Produce AI-generated climate advisories.
- Export analytical reports.
- Provide statistical insights with acceptable performance.

---

# Constraints

- Must use free and open-source technologies.
- Must support local development.
- Must remain modular and scalable.
- Must use publicly available environmental datasets.
- AI models should run locally where possible.
- Architecture should support future expansion.

---

# Risks

- Limited availability of high-resolution environmental datasets.
- API rate limits.
- Data quality inconsistencies.
- Large geospatial datasets impacting performance.
- Hardware limitations for local AI inference.

---

# Assumptions

- Environmental datasets remain publicly accessible.
- Weather and ocean APIs provide reliable updates.
- Users have internet access for fetching live environmental data.
- The MVP will initially focus on a single coastal region before expanding.
